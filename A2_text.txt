5Task

WHAT: Implement an EA, built on ariel.ec, that evolves the weights of a neural-network controller so a fixed body from the 'John Set' walks from its spawn position to a fixed target position. Investigate one aspect of your EA (e.g. mutation, crossover, parent selection, survivor selection, population size, or genotype representation) as your research question.
Body: free choice from the 'John Set' (see Supplementary material above). Fixed for your whole assignment.
World: free choice from ariel.simulation.environments, fixed for your whole assignment, with one exception: SimpleTiltedWorld is not supported for this task - bodies from the 'John Set' slide off the platform under a neutral gait before reaching the far side. SimpleFlatWorld, RuggedTerrainWorld, CraterTerrainWorld, AmphitheatreTerrainWorld, and OlympicArena are all valid choices.
Controller: a neural network whose weights you evolve, see A2_template_2026.py. CPG-based controllers are not supported for this task.
Fitness: the Euclidean distance, in the flat ground plane, between the core body's final position and a fixed target position (lower is better). Think carefully about what information your controller needs to solve this - a controller with no signal telling it where the target is, or nothing to drive rhythmic movement with, has very little to work with. ariel.simulation.tasks.targeted_locomotion has several ready-made variants if you want to build on one for your research question: plain remaining distance, distance reduced from the starting position (so a robot that starts closer is not rewarded for standing still), distance combined with a control-effort penalty, a version that penalises falling over, one that penalises a wandering path relative to a straight line, and one that rewards reaching the target quickly rather than just closely. You are free to argue for a different metric in your report, as long as you define it clearly.
HOW: Design an experiment to answer your research question. Repeat your final experiments at least 5 times independently and present statistics (mean and spread) based on these runs. Compare against a baseline (e.g. random search, or a fixed non-evolved controller) at the same evaluation budget.
WHAT TO HAND IN: The report as specified below and your code.
HOW TO HAND IN: Submit a single compressed file named groupnumber.zip, containing a folder with the same name, including the report as groupnumber.pdf and your code.

You are NOT allowed to make changes to the source code of the ariel framework (anything in src/ariel). Making such changes would be qualified as fraud, leading you to FAIL the assignment.

Compare your algorithm(s) with a line-plot across generations, showing the average/std of fitness over your independent runs.

8Tips

Test your algorithm first using a small population and short simulation durations.
This is a complex search problem - do not expect convergence in a handful of
generations. Run until your fitness curve plateaus, and treat that plateau, not a fixed
generation count, as your stopping criterion.
Physics-based fitness evaluation is much slower than pure body-scoring - budget your
time accordingly, and test your pipeline at a small scale before committing to a full run.
Be organized concerning the output files of your experiments.
Parametrize your seed (e.g. a command-line argument or environment variable) so running
your final ≥5-seed repeats is a single script invocation per configuration, not a manual
edit-and-rerun each time.
